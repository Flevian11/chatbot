# Learning Methods and Academic Skills
Document type: Educational reference
Scope: General study support; not an official LWF assessment policy.

## Effective learning
Active recall asks a learner to retrieve information from memory rather than reread it. Spaced practice revisits material over increasing intervals. Interleaving mixes related problem types to improve discrimination. Elaboration explains why a concept works and connects it to prior knowledge. Worked examples are useful early; gradually remove scaffolding as competence improves.

## Planning study
Break broad outcomes into observable tasks. Estimate effort, schedule focused sessions, include breaks and reserve time for review. Prioritise work by deadline, importance, dependencies and current understanding. A study plan should be realistic and revised using actual progress rather than guilt or arbitrary hours.

## Understanding difficult concepts
Start with the question the concept answers. Define key terms, identify assumptions, work through a small example, explain the mechanism in your own words and test the explanation against a counterexample. When stuck, identify the exact step where understanding breaks down.

## Problem solving
Clarify the goal, list known information and constraints, decompose the problem, propose an approach, test a small case, inspect results and refine. For programming, write expected input/output examples before implementation. For mathematics, show intermediate steps and check units, signs and boundary cases.

## Academic integrity
Use sources honestly, distinguish quotation from paraphrase and cite work that informed an answer according to the institution's rules. Do not submit another person's work as your own. AI can support explanation, feedback, practice questions and planning, but learners remain responsible for original assessed work and must follow their institution's policy.

## Feedback and reflection
Feedback is most useful when converted into a specific next action. Identify the criterion, the gap, the likely cause and a practice task. Reflection should focus on evidence and adjustments, not merely whether a session felt productive.
